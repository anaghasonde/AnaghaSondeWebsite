Most people are asked to choose a lane. I never could. I've spent my career architecting software systems for companies that needed things to work at scale — and in parallel, I've spent years sitting across from people untangling what isn't working in their lives, writing about my own unraveling and rebuilding, and dancing because some things can only be said with the body.

About Me

The long way around, on purpose

A journey across technology, consulting, healing, writing and dance — told the way it actually happened, not the way a resume would tell it.

I didn't plan this life. I built it one honest decision at a time.

I started in software the way a lot of engineers do — curious about how things worked, good at solving problems that had clean edges. Over the years, as I moved from writing code to architecting systems and leading technical teams, I noticed something: the hardest problems in any system were never really about the system. They were about the people building it, using it, depending on it. Architecture, I learned, is as much about people as it is about servers.

I stopped trying to keep the different parts of myself in separate rooms. It turns out they were always talking to each other.

Somewhere in the middle of a demanding technical career, my own life asked for an audit. I went through a period I now write about openly — one that took apart most of what I thought I knew about who I was. Coming back from that, slowly and without a map, is what led me to counselling and coaching. Not as a pivot away from technology, but as an addition to it. I understand systems under pressure. I understand people under pressure too. The training was different; the underlying skill — staying calm, diagnosing carefully, rebuilding thoughtfully — was the same.

Writing "Welcome Back to Yourself" was the natural next step. I needed to say, in full sentences and without editing myself for anyone's comfort, what that unraveling and rebuilding actually felt like. And dance — Bollywood first, belly dance later — has been the one part of my life that never needed to justify itself. It's the practice I return to when I want to feel like a body again, not just a mind managing several careers.

Today I still architect software. I still coach. I still write. I still dance, and I teach others to. This site is an honest map of all four — not a highlight reel, but a real one.

Architecture thinking

How I approach a system

Good architecture is a series of decisions you can defend two years later, under different requirements than the ones you started with. I design for change: services with clear boundaries, contracts that don't leak implementation detail, and infrastructure that scales without demanding a rewrite every time traffic doubles.

I care about the boring things that make systems trustworthy — observability, failure isolation, sane deployment pipelines — as much as the exciting things, like where AI and LLM integration can genuinely improve a product rather than just decorate it. And because I've led technical teams, I design with the humans who'll maintain the system in mind, not just the humans who'll launch it.

Mind Matters

Coaching and counselling for people who are ready to stop managing their lives and start actually living them.

My approach

Grounded, not prescriptive

I don't believe in handing people a five-step plan to a life they haven't examined. My approach starts with listening long enough to understand what's actually happening beneath the presenting problem — the job stress that's really a boundaries issue, the relationship conflict that's really an old wound repeating itself.

I bring the same diagnostic patience I use in technical architecture to this work: understand the real system before proposing a fix. Sessions are collaborative, honest, and unhurried. I'll ask the direct question when it needs asking, and I'll sit with you in the harder moments without rushing you toward resolution.

Coaching & counselling

How we work together

I offer both structured coaching engagements — for people working toward a specific change — and open-ended counselling conversations for people who need space more than a plan. Sessions are one-on-one, held over video or in person, and paced to what you actually need rather than a fixed program.

Most people start with a single conversation. There's no obligation to continue, and no pressure to have your problem "figured out" before you show up.

Dance

Bollywood & Belly Dance

The part of my life that has never needed a business case — performed, taught, and lived in the body.

Dance is the one place all my other titles disappear.

I grew up around Bollywood dance — its storytelling, its unapologetic expressiveness, its refusal to be subtle when subtlety isn't the point. Belly dance came later, and taught me something different: precision, isolation, the discipline of a body that knows exactly what each muscle is doing while still looking effortless.

Together, they've become a practice I return to constantly — as a performer, and increasingly as a teacher for others discovering the same joy.

My dance journey

From childhood Bollywood routines at family functions to formal training in belly dance technique, my journey has been about following what felt alive, not what looked impressive on paper. I still train regularly, and I still get nervous before a performance — which I've decided is the correct amount of respect to have for this art form.

Performance
